\begin{abstract}
This proposal outlines a phase-two expansion of the \textit{Differential Geometry of Prompt Space} framework. While prior work established the latent space of Large Language Models (LLMs) as a differentiable manifold $\manifold$, this research aims to formalize the navigation of that manifold. By integrating Riemannian metrics, parallel transport, and holonomy, we propose a rigorous calculus to model context stability, semantic distance, and the high-curvature transitions associated with emergent model behaviors and ``shocks.''
\end{abstract}


\section{Introduction}
The rapid advancement of transformer-based architectures has necessitated a shift from heuristic prompt engineering to a formal mathematical treatment of LLM state transitions. Following the foundations laid in \textit{Prompt Algebra} (2025) and \textit{Differential Geometry of Prompt Space} (2026), this proposal seeks to define the dynamic laws governing prompt-response trajectories.


\section{Research Objectives}
The primary objective is to transition from a descriptive model to a \textbf{predictive calculus} of LLM behavior. Key goals include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Metric Formalization:} Define a Riemannian metric to quantify the ``semantic effort'' required for state transitions.
    \item \textbf{Context Stability Modeling:} Utilize the Levi-Civita connection and parallel transport to measure how instructions (tangent vectors) evolve or decay over long sequences.
    \item \textbf{Curvature Analysis:} Characterize regions of the manifold via sectional curvature to distinguish stability zones from divergence zones.
\end{itemize}


\section{Theoretical Framework}


\subsection{The Latent Manifold and Riemannian Metric}
Let $\promptspace$ denote the space of prompts and $\phi: \promptspace \to \mathbb{R}^n$ an embedding map into a latent vector space. We model the prompt space as a differentiable manifold $\manifold$. We define a Riemannian metric $g$ induced by the embedding:
\begin{equation}
    g_p(u, v) = \langle D\phi_p(u), D\phi_p(v) \rangle
\end{equation}
where $u, v \in \tangent$ and $D\phi_p$ is the differential of the embedding map. We define the ``cost'' or semantic action $S$ along a path $\gamma$ as:
\begin{equation}
    S[\gamma] = \int_0^1 \sqrt{g_{\gamma(t)}(\dot{\gamma}(t), \dot{\gamma}(t))} \, dt
\end{equation}


\subsection{Parallel Transport and the Connection}
To address the phenomenon of context drift, we model a system prompt as a tangent vector $v \in \tangent$. As a response sequence unfolds along a trajectory $\gamma(t)$, the evolution of $v$ is governed by the covariant derivative:
\begin{equation}
    \nabla_{\dot{\gamma}(t)} v = 0
\end{equation}
A non-zero covariant derivative represents the degree to which a model deviates from its initial constraints.


\subsection{Semantic Holonomy}
We propose measuring model bias via holonomy along a closed loop $\gamma$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{H}_\gamma(v) = v' - v
\end{equation}
Where $v'$ is the vector returned after transportation. This provides a measurable quantity for semantic distortion in cyclic tasks.


\section{Methodology}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Metric Approximation:} Estimating local geometry via KL-divergence and cosine similarity proxies for $g_{ij}$.
    \item \textbf{Curvature Mapping:} Utilizing \textit{Orthogonal Shock Operators} to identify high-curvature zones associated with affective or creative emergence.
    \item \textbf{Geodesic Verification:} Comparing geodesic paths against linear interpolations to verify logical coherence in state transitions.
\end{enumerate}


\section{Conclusion}
This framework moves LLM interaction from a heuristic art to a formal branch of functional geometry, enabling deterministic prompting and mathematical control over model emergence.
